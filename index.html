Module 1 — Foundations: What a Number Means

Episode 1

What a Number Means

"We scored 70%." What does that even mean? Mean, median, spread, and the histogram that shows the truth: a contract-review build that aces easy NDAs and fails hard MSAs outright — while the average looks fine. Then a second build with the same average and a completely different spread, and what the spread says about the next document. And the reversal: on the equal mix the first build wins, but weighted by the department's real portfolio the second build wins — the headline depends on the mix.

Episode 2

Base Rates and Bayes

The model says 90% likely. Likely compared to what? The 73.6% dismissal base rate, and why a model that always predicts "dismissed" scores 73.6% without knowing anything. The sufficiency check as a Bayesian update.

6:34

Episode 3

How Do You Build a Test Set?

Before the statistics, the test set. A biased test set makes every number after it meaningless — no formula fixes a test set that does not look like the work.

6:40

Episode 4

Why More Cases Help

Why is the mean of 50 questions steadier than one question? Sampling distributions, standard error, and the Central Limit Theorem — the whole reason test sets work.

6:30

Episode 5

Pass or Fail Is a Weighted Coin

7 out of 10 — good or not? The binomial distribution, and why the same count can come from a 50% build and an 80% build.

6:40

Module 2 — The Inference Toolkit

Episode 6

How Sure Are You?

7 out of 10 is really 40–89%. The Wilson interval, why the Wald interval is wrong at small n, and the 4x rule. Report the interval, never the bare percentage.

6:56

Episode 7

What Does p Mean?

The vendor says p = 0.03. What are they actually claiming? A single proportion against a threshold: 68 passes out of 100 against a claimed 75% — p ≈ 0.069, not enough evidence to reject the claim, and the interval shows why.

6:38

Episode 8

What Did the Tool Miss?

70% accurate — but what did it miss, and what did it flag for nothing? The confusion matrix, precision, recall, and why accuracy hides the asymmetry.

7:13

Episode 9

How Many Cases Do You Need?

"How many cases?" is three questions, not one. Power, sample-size tables, and why a 5-point improvement needs roughly 625 total paired cases — plus the correction that significance is the wrong goal for a ten-question eval.

6:37

Episode 10

How Big Is the Difference?

A 2-point gain on 2,000 cases can be significant and worthless. Effect size, the minimum important difference set by the department, and non-inferiority — is the cheaper build no worse within a stated margin?

6:40

Module 3 — Comparing Builds

Episode 11

The Paired t-Test

Build A averages 0.82, Build B 0.76. Is the gap real? The paired t-test on mean rubric scores — 25 contracts, t = 3.1, p ≈ 0.005 — and when to use it instead of McNemar.

6:51

Episode 12

Did the Fix Help?

Same questions, new prompt. McNemar's exact test and the paired bootstrap — and why pairing buys power over unpaired comparison.

7:00

Episode 13

Your Items Are Not Independent

100 questions from 10 cases behave like about 36. Clustering, the design effect, and why the headline n overstates your confidence.

6:50

Module 4 — Scoring and Agreement

Episode 14

Scoring Probabilities

The model says 90% confident. Is it right 90% of the time? The Brier score, calibration, ECE, and the 90% bucket — Fable got 81 out of 81 right; Gemini got 57 of 320 wrong.

7:16

Episode 15

Do the Graders Agree?

Two partners graded the same outputs. 80% raw agreement can mean κ = 0.375. Cohen's κ, Fleiss κ, weighted κ, ICC, Krippendorff's α — and why agreement is not correctness.

7:12

Episode 16

Did the Triage Check Work?

A check has two error rates, not one. TPR, FPR, balanced accuracy — Luna catches 89.3% but cries wolf 64.1% of the time — and non-conclusions as a third outcome.

6:43

Episode 17

What the Model Asks Matters More

Models that ask more do better. Or do they? Variable coverage correlates with score at r = 0.73; clarification rate only at r = 0.40. Pearson's r, and why a correlation is not a cause.

6:45

Module 5 — Reporting Your Results

Episode 18

How Not to Fool Yourself

We ran five tests and one came out significant. Did we find something? P-hacking, multiple comparisons, and deciding the tests before you look.

7:08

Episode 19

Reporting Your Results (Series Finale)

The finale. Eighteen episodes of statistics condensed into the one-page eval report: score, interval, n, test, effect, cost, limits, version. Post-deployment monitoring, workflow evaluation, and the cautionary tales — a report that hides its limits is a demo with better stationery.
